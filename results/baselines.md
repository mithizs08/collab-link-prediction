# Heuristic baselines

_Generated by `scripts/run_baselines.py` from `results/baselines.csv`, `results/baselines_paired.csv` and `results/katz_beta_selection.csv`. Values: point estimate [bootstrap 95% CI, 1,000 resamples]._

Truncated Katz uses beta = 0.005, chosen on validation (hard protocol, pooled ROC-AUC); see the selection table at the end. `prefer_newer` is a reference row, not a heuristic: −min(works before the cutoff) of the two authors, i.e. it only prefers pairs with a newer author and shows how much a protocol rewards newcomer status.

Ranking metrics use expected ranks under random tie-breaking (see `linkpred.eval.metrics`). A random ranker scores MRR ≈ 0.051 with 100 candidates; query_auc 0.5.

**Reading guide.** Neighbourhood heuristics (CN, Jaccard, Adamic-Adar, resource allocation) score every pair without a common neighbour exactly 0. Almost no random negative or random ranking candidate has a common neighbour, so under `random` and `rank_random` these heuristics essentially test *whether a positive is at distance 2*. `hard` and `rank_hard` compare distance-2 pairs only; `hard_active` / `rank_hard_active` also restrict negatives to authors who publish in the prediction year (Phase 4). From 2026-10-15 the pair-level protocols are headlined by the **macro within-group** score: positives and negatives are compared inside the established and newcomer groups separately and the two scores are averaged, because pooled numbers are affected by the newcomer imbalance between positives and negatives (audit M2, `docs/decisions.md`).
