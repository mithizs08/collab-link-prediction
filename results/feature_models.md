**How to read this.** `hard_active` (distance-2 positives vs hard negatives whose two authors both publish in the prediction year) is the lead pair-level metric: it asks *which pairs collaborate* among people who are all still publishing. `hard` keeps negatives with inactive authors and therefore *includes activity*: a model can score well on it by predicting who keeps publishing (Phase 4 finding, docs/decisions.md). Activity is used only to select evaluation pairs, never as a model input.

## Choices made on validation

- Negative mix: logistic regression **hard fraction 0, 1:1**; LightGBM **hard fraction 0, 1:3** (rule: best val `hard` ROC-AUC among mixes with val rank_random MRR ≥ 90% of the best mix).
- LightGBM: num_leaves 7, learning_rate 0.03, min_child_samples 100, subsample 0.8, colsample_bytree 0.8; early stopping on val hard AP.
- Pair-focused LightGBM: training negatives = activity-matched hard negatives from the 2023 window (1:3); num_leaves 7, learning_rate 0.03, min_child_samples 20; early stopping on val hard_active AP, selected by val hard_active ROC-AUC.
- Recency half-life: 2 years (val hard ROC-AUC of recency-weighted Adamic-Adar alone).
- Validation-best heuristic per protocol (paired-test reference): hard_active: adamic_adar, rank_hard_active: jaccard, hard: adamic_adar, rank_hard: katz, rank_random: katz, random: katz; Adamic-Adar is also tested on the four pair-level protocols.

## Appendix: diagnostic_all_vs_hard

> **Warning — diagnostic only, not a result.** All hard negatives are at distance 2, while 34–41% of positives are at distance ≥ 3, so in this comparison distance alone separates the classes. A learned model that picks up distance (or anything correlated with it) can look good here for the wrong reason; neighbourhood heuristics are pushed the other way, because they score every distance-3+ positive 0, below every hard negative. Neither direction says anything about the task. Use `hard` instead.
