# Final summary (Phase 7)

_Generated by `scripts/final_summary.py`; every number is read from the results files named in each section. Test split unless stated; validation numbers are in the same files (and `results/final_comparison.md`)._

**Protocols.** Headline = macro within-group score: positives and negatives / candidates are compared inside the established group (no author with ≤ 2 works before the cutoff) and the newcomer group separately, and the two scores are averaged. `hard_active`: distance-2 positives vs distance-2 non-collaborators who both publish in the prediction year (ROC-AUC). `rank_hard_active`: each distance-2 positive ranked against up to 100 active distance-2 candidates (per-query AUC). Pooled `hard` does not match activity, so it rewards predicting who keeps publishing, and it is affected by newcomer imbalance. `random`: all positives vs random UTS pairs. Learned models: mean over 5 seeds [seed-mean bootstrap 95% CI] ±seed sd; 5,000 resamples.

**Caveat (audit M1 / N3).** The saved GNNs were trained on Colab before the distance-2 fix: their pair-focused training positives include pairs at distance ≥ 3 while every negative is at distance 2. `gnn_pair_focused_sage_structural` learned that shortcut (shortcut check 0.775 ± 0.006: test ROC-AUC of far random negatives against distance-2 positives, `results/gnn_shortcut_check.csv`; above 0.5 = it ranks far non-collaborators above near collaborators; the retrained pair-focused LightGBM scores 0.015). The hybrid adds this GNN's out-of-fold score to the retrained pair-focused LightGBM, so it is built on a shortcut-trained model and is a secondary result. The GNNs were not retrained (no Colab rerun); `scripts/run_gnn.py` now trains distance-2 positives only.

## 4. Findings

The project's three research questions were implicit in the plan; they are stated explicitly here. **RQ1:** under leak-free, activity- and newcomer-matched evaluation, do learned models beat graph heuristics? **RQ2:** what makes a new collaboration predictable (structure, activity, text, newcomer status)? **RQ3:** do learned representations (node2vec, GNNs) add anything over engineered pair features?

1. **RQ1 — yes, but modestly.** The pair-focused LightGBM is the best model: macro hard_active 0.799 and macro rank_hard_active 0.792, vs Adamic-Adar 0.725 / 0.699 (difference +0.074 [+0.058, +0.091], Holm p = 0.0048; +0.093 [+0.081, +0.106], Holm p = 0.0048). It also beats the general LightGBM (+0.048 [+0.036, +0.060], Holm p = 0.0048). In absolute terms, a ROC-AUC of about 0.80 among friend-of-friend pairs who are both active means about one in five comparisons is ranked the wrong way round: it is useful for ranking candidates, not for predicting individual collaborations.
2. **The general models gain little over a one-line heuristic once activity is matched.** Logistic regression 0.763 and general LightGBM 0.751 vs Adamic-Adar 0.725 (LightGBM − AA +0.027 [+0.009, +0.044], Holm p = 0.0060). The same general LightGBM scores 0.864 on pooled hard, which does not match activity: much of its apparent skill there is predicting who keeps publishing.
3. **RQ2 — activity and newcomer status inflate the easy protocols; no single feature family carries the pair signal.** In the general-LightGBM ablation (`results/ablation.csv`), temporal (activity) features alone reach 0.840 macro hard but only 0.707 macro hard_active; text alone is the only family that barely drop (0.732 → 0.727); structure alone gives 0.701. Once activity is matched the single families are within 0.03 of each other and all features together reach 0.751: topical similarity and network position each add a little, none dominates. The prefer_newer reference, which uses no pair information, scores 0.647 on pooled hard_active but 0.578 macro: the pooled number rewards newcomer composition alone.
4. **Modelling activity explicitly does not help.** The two-stage model (P(both active) × pair-focused LightGBM) scores 0.854 on pooled hard vs 0.864 for the general LightGBM (-0.010 [-0.016, -0.005], Holm p = 0.0048), although its stage-1 activity model is good on its own task (section 3.3). The general LightGBM already learns activity and its interaction with the pair signal; the product of two separately trained probabilities loses that. It is also worse than the pair-focused model on macro hard_active (0.698).
5. **RQ3 — no. Representation learning lost to engineered features and to Adamic-Adar.** node2vec 0.561 and the best GNN 0.640 on macro hard_active are both below Adamic-Adar (GNN − AA -0.084 [-0.114, -0.053], Holm p = 0.0048); the GNN was trained with the distance shortcut (check 0.775), so its true ceiling is unknown without retraining. Adding its out-of-fold score to the pair-focused LightGBM (the hybrid, secondary) changes macro hard_active by +0.009 [+0.005, +0.014], Holm p = 0.0048 and macro rank_hard_active by +0.006 [+0.002, +0.010], Holm p = 0.0144: at most a hundredth of AUC, from a shortcut-trained model.
6. **The pair-focused model is a specialist.** On the random protocol it scores 0.912 vs 0.982 for the general LightGBM and 0.829 for Adamic-Adar: it ranks pairs within the friend-of-friend, both-active setting it was trained for; a deployed recommender would need something else (e.g. the general model) to shortlist candidates first.
7. **The main conclusion survives the robustness checks, which are themselves limited.** With the flagged possible-duplicate author IDs removed, the pair-focused LightGBM still beats Adamic-Adar (0.780 vs 0.710, +0.071 [+0.049, +0.095], Holm p = 0.0048; rank: +0.090 [+0.070, +0.109], Holm p = 0.0048), but the heuristic flag removes 46% of the test distance-2 positives, so this is a check on a much smaller, different population, not a correction. Extending the pair-focused grid changed the selected parameters by a noise-level validation margin and left test macro hard_active at 0.799.
